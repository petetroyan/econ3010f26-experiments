The Lowest Unique Number Game

Everyone picked a whole number from 1 to 99. The winner was whoever picked the lowest number that nobody else picked, the LUPI (lowest unique positive integer). Low numbers are tempting, which is exactly why they get crowded.

The prediction row is the equilibrium of the game for a class of 88: the mix of numbers where no single number gives anyone a better shot than another.

What happened

  • A lot of people picked 1 and 2, and eleven more picked something above 30.
  • In later rounds, fewer people rushed to the bottom. Picks of 1 alone dropped from 11 to 7 to 5. Similarly, there were fewer higher picks of numbers greater than 30, as the class learned that these are very unlikely to win.
  • Theory predicts an average near 11.6, which is close to the round 2 average in the class data. In Round 3, the class overshot a little bit and went lower, with an average of 7.9.
  • If we had played longer, we probably would have seen more convergence towards the predicted equilibrium (as we saw in the Swedish data).
