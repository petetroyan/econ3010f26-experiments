The Lowest Unique Number Game

Everyone picked a whole number from 1 to 99. The winner was whoever picked the lowest number that nobody else picked, the LUPI (lowest unique positive integer). Low numbers are tempting, which is exactly why they get crowded.

The prediction row is the equilibrium of the game for a class of 88: the mix of numbers where no single number gives anyone a better shot than another.

What happened

  • Round 1 was all over the place. Eleven people picked 1, and eleven more picked something above 30, where theory says almost nobody should go.
  • By round 3 the class had pulled in. The average pick fell from 16.4 to 7.9 and only two picks were above 30. Everyone learned that high numbers are wasted.
  • Then the class overshot. Crowding onto 4 and 6 left the low numbers so packed that the only unique number below 16 was 12. Theory predicts an average near 11.6, and the class ended below it.
  • This is what real players do too. When Sweden ran this as a national lottery in 2007, with about 54,000 bets a day, players started far from the prediction and moved toward it day by day, with nothing to go on but the winning number.
